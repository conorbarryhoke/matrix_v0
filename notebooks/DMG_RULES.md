# Rules for Creating Monsters from the DMG
## Damage Output
To determine a monster's overall damage output, take the average damage it deals with each of its attacks in a round and add them together. If a monster has different attack options, use the monster's most effective attacks to determinie its damage output. 

If a monster's damage output varies from round to round, calculate its damage output each round for the first 3 rounds of combat.

In the first three rounds of combat, a dragon will get to use its breath weapon once, and its multiattack routine twice.

Auras: Assume that one character in the party is within 5 feet at all times. Assume that 2 characters are within 15 feet.

Reactions: Assume they are used once per turn.


## Speed
Every monster has a walking speed. (Immobile monsters have a walking speed of 0 feet). In addition to its walking speed, a monster might have one or more other speeds, including a burrowing, climbing, flying, or swimming speed.

### Flying Monster
Increase the mosnter's effective Armor Class by 2 if it can fly and deal damage at range and if its expected challenge rating is 10 or lower.

## Saving THrow Bonuses
A monster with 3 or more saving throw bonuses has a significant defensive advantage, so its effective AC should be raised when determining its challenge rating. If it has 3 or 4 bonuses, increase its effective AC by 2. If it has five or more bonuses, increase its effective AC by 4.

## Skill Bonuses
Skill bonuses have no bearing on a monster's challenge rating.

## Senses
Whether the monster has aspecial senses or not has no bearing on its challenge rating. 

## Condition Immunities
THese immunities have no bearing on its challenge rating. They should be logical.

## Languages
Whether a monster can speak a language has no bearing on its challenge rating.

## final Challenge Rating
Average the monster's defensive challenge rating and its offensive challenge rating.
